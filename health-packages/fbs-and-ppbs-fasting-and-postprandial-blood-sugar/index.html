FBS & PPBS (Fasting & Postprandial Blood Sugar)

The FBS & PPBS (Fasting & Postprandial Blood Sugar) test is a group of blood tests that measure fasting blood glucose (FBG) after an overnight fast of 8–12 hours and postprandial blood glucose (PPBG) 2 hours after starting the meal. Together, these tests show how your body processes glucose both before and after meals, helping to predict the risk of diabetes and prediabetes, and monitor diabetes treatment. These tests are commonly included in routine health checkups and diabetes screening tests.

What does FBS & PPBS (Fasting & Postprandial Blood Sugar) measure?

Package contains 2 tests

  • PPBS (Postprandial Blood Sugar)
  • FBS (Fasting Blood Sugar)

Samples required

  • BloodOur phlebotomist will draw a blood sample, typically from a vein in your inner elbow.

Preparations

  • For FBS: Overnight fasting (8-12 hrs) is required. Do not eat or drink anything except water before the test.
  • For PPBS: Sample to be given exactly 2 hours after starting meal or as advised by the physician. Please note that it is mandatory to abide by the pre-scheduled sample collection time, as this will help us ensure the accuracy of the test results.
  • Note: You need to place the order only once for this test. Two sample collections will be scheduled. The first sample collection is for the Fasting Blood Sugar (FBS) sample, which will be collected at your scheduled time after the required fasting period. The second sample collection is for the Postprandial Blood Sugar (PPBS) sample and will be scheduled 2 hours after the first sample. Please have your meal after the first sample collection.

Why is this package booked?

  • To evaluate how the body regulates blood sugar before and after meals.
  • To detect diabetes, prediabetes, or impaired glucose tolerance.
  • To assess sensitivity to insulin and understand daily glucose fluctuations.
  • To monitor glucose control in individuals with diabetes or those on diabetes medications.
  • To help guide treatment plans, dietary changes, and lifestyle modifications.
  • To identify abnormal spikes or drops in blood sugar that may cause symptoms like fatigue, dizziness, or increased hunger.
  • To provide a complete picture of metabolic health for people undergoing weight-loss or GLP-1–based therapies.
