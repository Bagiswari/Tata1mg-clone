Cancer Marker Profile - Female

The Cancer Marker Profile - Female is a comprehensive female cancer marker package designed to support risk assessment, diagnosis support, and long-term monitoring of selected malignancies. The panel includes Alpha Feto protein (AFP), CA 15.3, CA 19.9, Carcino embryonic Antigen (CEA), Thyroglobulin, CA 125, Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH), CBC (Complete Blood Count), and ESR (Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate) tests. It measures multiple tumor markers and hematological parameters that help detect potential malignancy, assess disease burden, and monitor treatment response or recurrence, when interpreted alongside other clinical findings.

What does Cancer Marker Profile - Female measure?

Package contains 29 tests

  • CBC (Complete Blood Count) (21)
    • Differential Leukocyte Count (5)
    • Red Blood Cell Count
    • Hb (Hemoglobin)
    • Platelet Count
    • Total Leukocyte Count
    • Hematocrit
    • Mean Corpuscular Volume
    • Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin
    • Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin Concentration
    • Absolute Leucocyte Count (5)
    • Mean Platelet Volume
    • PDW
    • RDW CV
  • ESR (Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate)
  • Lactate Dehydrogenase, Serum
  • CA125 (Cancer Antigen 125)
  • CA15.3
  • Thyroglobulin
  • Carcino Embryonic Antigen Test
  • Alpha Feto Protein, Tumour Marker
  • CA19.9

Samples required

  • BloodOur phlebotomist will draw a blood sample, typically from a vein in your inner elbow.

Preparations

  • For AFP, CA 15.3, CA 19.9, CA 125 and CEA: Provide brief clinical history/doctor's prescription at the time of sample collection.
  • Test should not be done immediately before or during menstruation or immediately after abdominal surgery, as these cause transiently increased levels. Pregnancy may also affect the levels of CA 125.

Why is this package booked?

  • To evaluate multiple cancers commonly seen in females.
  • To support the diagnosis of suspected malignancy.
  • To monitor the response to cancer treatment.
  • To detect recurrence of cancer after successful treatment.
  • To track the progression of known cancers.
  • To assess unexplained symptoms such as weight loss, fatigue, pain, or abnormal bleeding.
  • To evaluate overall blood health and organ involvement.
  • If there is a personal or family history of cancer
