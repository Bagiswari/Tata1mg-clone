Routine Blood Tests List: Names, What They Check and When to Get Them

By Tata 1mg Labs · 21 November 2026 · 4 min read

Routine blood tests are the tests doctors order most often, at a yearly checkup, before a procedure or to follow up on symptoms. They are quick, need only a small blood sample and tell you a lot about how your body is working. Here is a routine blood tests list with the name of each test, what it checks and when you might need it.

What Each Group of Tests Tells You

  • Blood count (CBC): the starting point of most checkups. It can show anaemia, infection or a platelet problem.
  • Blood sugar (FBS and HbA1c): FBS gives a snapshot of today; HbA1c shows the average of the last few months. Together they screen for diabetes and prediabetes. See our guide on which blood tests need fasting.
  • Lipid profile: one of the main checks for heart disease risk.
  • Liver and kidney tests: show how well these organs are working and can pick up early stress, for example from fatty liver. Our article on the SGPT test normal range explains one of the most common liver results.
  • Thyroid profile: checks for an underactive or overactive thyroid. Read our thyroid test guide to understand T3, T4 and TSH.
  • Vitamins: Vitamin D and B12 deficiencies are very common in India and are easy to correct once found.

How Often Should You Get Routine Blood Tests?

For most healthy adults, a yearly set of routine blood tests is a good habit. If you have diabetes, a thyroid condition, high cholesterol or take long-term medicines, your doctor may ask you to repeat certain tests every three to six months. Frequently Asked Questions

What are the most common routine blood tests?

The most common routine blood tests are CBC, fasting blood sugar, HbA1c, lipid profile, liver function test, kidney function test, thyroid profile, Vitamin D and Vitamin B12, often with a urine routine test.

Which routine blood tests need fasting?

Fasting blood sugar and lipid profile need 10–12 hours of fasting. CBC, HbA1c, thyroid profile and Vitamin D do not.

How often should I get routine blood tests?

Once a year is a good routine for most healthy adults. People with a long-term condition may need some tests every three to six months, as advised by their doctor.

Can routine blood tests be done at home?

Yes. Routine blood tests and most pathology tests on a blood or urine sample can be done with home sample collection, at no extra charge.
