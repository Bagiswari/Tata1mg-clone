SGPT Test Normal Range: What High ALT Levels Mean

By Tata 1mg Labs · 27 October 2026 · 4 min read

SGPT is one of the most-searched results on a liver function test report, and also one of the most commonly raised. If your report shows a high SGPT value, it is usually a sign to look more closely at your liver, not a cause for panic. Here is what the SGPT test measures, the normal range, and what different levels can mean.

What Is the SGPT Test?

SGPT stands for Serum Glutamic Pyruvic Transaminase, also called ALT (Alanine Aminotransferase). It is an enzyme found mainly inside liver cells. When liver cells are irritated or damaged, ALT leaks into the blood, so a raised SGPT is one of the earliest signs of liver stress.

SGPT is usually tested as part of a Liver Function Test (LFT), alongside SGOT (AST), bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase, GGT and proteins.

SGPT Normal Range

Reference ranges vary between labs and testing methods, so always compare your result with the range printed on your own report. As a general guide:

GroupTypical SGPT (ALT) normal range
Adult menAbout 7–50 U/L
Adult womenAbout 7–35 U/L
ChildrenVaries with age; check the lab's age-specific range

U/L means units per litre. Some labs and liver specialists use lower upper limits (for example, around 30–33 U/L for men and 19–25 U/L for women) to catch early liver problems.

What Do High SGPT Levels Mean?

  • Mildly raised (up to about 2–3 times the upper limit): very common. Often linked to fatty liver, alcohol, being overweight, certain medicines or recent intense exercise.
  • Moderately raised (about 3–10 times): may point to ongoing liver inflammation, such as alcohol-related liver disease, chronic viral hepatitis or medicine-related injury.
  • Very high (more than 10 times): suggests acute liver injury, for example acute viral hepatitis or a reaction to a medicine or toxin. This needs prompt medical attention.

Common Causes of High SGPT

  • Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) — the most common cause, linked to excess weight, diabetes and high triglycerides.
  • Alcohol — regular drinking stresses liver cells.
  • Viral hepatitis — hepatitis A, B, C or E.
  • Medicines — including high doses of paracetamol, some antibiotics, anti-TB drugs, statins and certain supplements.
  • Muscle injury or very hard exercise — can raise ALT slightly.
  • Other conditions — such as thyroid disorders, celiac disease or rare inherited conditions.

SGOT/SGPT Ratio

Doctors often compare SGOT (AST) with SGPT (ALT). In fatty liver and viral hepatitis, SGPT is usually higher than SGOT. A ratio of SGOT to SGPT above 2 is often seen in alcohol-related liver disease. The ratio is a clue, not a diagnosis.

Symptoms to Watch For

Raised SGPT often causes no symptoms at all, which is why it is usually found on a routine checkup. See a doctor promptly if you notice yellowing of the eyes or skin, dark urine, pale stools, pain in the upper right abdomen, ongoing nausea or unusual tiredness.

How to Bring SGPT Down

  • Lose weight gradually if you are overweight — even 5–10% of body weight can improve fatty liver.
  • Cut down on or stop alcohol.
  • Reduce sugary drinks, refined carbohydrates and fried food.
  • Stay active with regular exercise.
  • Review your medicines and supplements with your doctor — don't stop prescribed medicines on your own.
  • Repeat the test as advised, usually after a few weeks to months, to see the trend.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the normal range of SGPT?

SGPT (ALT) is typically about 7–50 U/L in adult men and 7–35 U/L in adult women, but ranges vary by lab. Always check the reference range printed on your report.

Is SGPT 60 dangerous?

An SGPT of around 60 U/L is mildly raised and is commonly linked to fatty liver, alcohol, medicines or recent exercise. It is not usually an emergency, but you should discuss it with your doctor and repeat the test as advised.

Is SGPT the same as ALT?

Yes. SGPT (Serum Glutamic Pyruvic Transaminase) and ALT (Alanine Aminotransferase) are two names for the same liver enzyme.

Do I need to fast for an SGPT test?

Fasting is not strictly needed for SGPT alone, but many labs recommend an overnight fast for a full liver function test or a health package.
