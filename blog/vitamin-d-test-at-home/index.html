Vitamin D Test at Home: Normal Range, Price and Who Should Get Tested

By Tata 1mg Labs · 27 October 2026 · 4 min read

India gets plenty of sunshine, yet Vitamin D deficiency is surprisingly common across age groups. Long hours indoors, pollution, covered clothing and darker skin all reduce how much Vitamin D the skin can make. Because the symptoms are vague, many people don't know they are low until they get tested. The good news: a Vitamin D test needs just one blood sample, and you can have it collected at home.

What the Vitamin D Test Measures

The standard test is 25-hydroxy Vitamin D, written as 25-OH Vitamin D or Vitamin D (25-OH). It is the main storage form of Vitamin D in the blood and the best reflection of your overall Vitamin D status over the past few weeks.

Vitamin D Normal Range

25-OH Vitamin D levelWhat it usually means
Below 20 ng/mLDeficient
20–29 ng/mLInsufficient
30–100 ng/mLSufficient
Above 100 ng/mLPossibly too high; risk of toxicity at very high levels

Some reports use nmol/L instead of ng/mL. To convert, multiply ng/mL by 2.5 (so 30 ng/mL equals 75 nmol/L). Cut-offs differ slightly between guidelines and labs, so check the range on your report.

Signs of Vitamin D Deficiency

  • Constant tiredness or low energy
  • Bone pain, lower back pain or aching joints
  • Muscle weakness or cramps
  • Frequent infections
  • Low mood
  • Hair fall
  • Slow wound healing

Many people with low Vitamin D have no symptoms at all, which is why testing matters.

Who Should Get a Vitamin D Test?

  • People who spend most of the day indoors, such as office workers and students
  • Older adults, whose skin makes less Vitamin D
  • People with darker skin, or who cover most of their skin outdoors
  • Pregnant and breastfeeding women
  • People who are overweight
  • Anyone with osteoporosis, frequent fractures or bone pain
  • People with conditions that affect absorption, such as celiac disease, inflammatory bowel disease, or liver or kidney disease
  • Anyone taking Vitamin D supplements, to check that the dose is working and not too high

How to Prepare

No fasting is needed for a Vitamin D test, so you can book any time slot. If you take Vitamin D supplements, keep taking them as usual and tell your doctor the dose. If the test is part of a package with fasting tests, follow the package's fasting instructions — see which blood tests need fasting.

After Your Result

If your level is low, your doctor may advise more safe sun exposure, Vitamin D-rich foods (such as eggs, oily fish and fortified milk) and supplements at the right dose. Don't start high-dose supplements without medical advice, as too much Vitamin D can cause harm. A repeat test after about 3 months of treatment shows whether your level has improved.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the normal range for Vitamin D?

A 25-OH Vitamin D level of 30–100 ng/mL is generally considered sufficient. Below 20 ng/mL is deficient and 20–29 ng/mL is insufficient, though cut-offs vary slightly between labs.

Do I need to fast for a Vitamin D test?

No. A Vitamin D test does not need fasting and can be done at any time of day.

How long does a Vitamin D report take?

Vitamin D reports are usually ready within 24 hours and are shared by email and WhatsApp.

Can I get a Vitamin D test at home?

Yes. A trained phlebotomist can collect your blood sample at home at no extra charge, and the sample is tested at a certified partner lab.
