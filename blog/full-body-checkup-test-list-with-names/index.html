Full Body Checkup Test List with Names: What Each Test Checks

By Tata 1mg Labs · 27 October 2026 · 4 min read

When you book a full body checkup, the package page usually says something like "Contains 86 tests". But which tests are they, and what does each one actually check? This guide gives you the full body checkup test list with names, grouped by the part of the body each test looks at, so you can read your package details and your report with confidence.

Why a Full Body Checkup Has So Many "Tests"

The headline number counts parameters, not separate blood draws. A single Complete Blood Count, for example, reports around 21 parameters from one sample tube. A typical full body checkup needs only one blood sample and one urine sample, collected together.

Advanced Tests in Comprehensive Packages

Higher-tier full body checkups add markers that look more closely at heart, metabolic and immune health:

  • Lipoprotein(a) and Apolipoprotein A1 & B — inherited and particle-level heart risk markers.
  • hsCRP and homocysteine — inflammation and blood-vessel health markers linked to heart risk.
  • High Sensitive Troponin-I — a heart muscle marker.
  • HOMA-IR — insulin resistance, an early warning sign before diabetes.
  • Amylase and lipase — pancreas enzymes.
  • Total IgE — a general allergy marker.
  • Cortisol — the body's main stress hormone.
  • Rheumatoid factor — a marker used in assessing joint pain.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many tests are there in a full body checkup?

It depends on the package. Basic full body checkups usually report around 60–75 parameters, while comprehensive packages report 85 to over 100. The count includes every parameter in panels like CBC and LFT, not separate blood draws.

Which tests are included in a basic full body checkup?

A basic package usually includes CBC, fasting blood sugar, HbA1c, lipid profile, liver function test, kidney function test, thyroid profile and urine routine.

Does a full body checkup need fasting?

Yes, most do. Fasting blood sugar and lipid profile need a 10–12 hour fast, so plan an early-morning sample collection.

Is one blood sample enough for all the tests?

Yes. A trained phlebotomist collects a few small tubes of blood in one draw, along with a urine sample, which covers all the tests in the package.
